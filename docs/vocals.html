Vocals: Lead, Screams, Stacks

Goal

Clarity first. A dead-mono lead core sits in front of Drop G–Bb guitars. BGVs wrap wide around it inside vocal_master.

Every vocal send lands on a return inside vocal_master. Never send a vocal to fx_hall: it lives in instrument_bus, and its vocal tail would stay in the instrumental print.

HPF before Coffee The Pun

Always put an 85–100 Hz HPF before Coffee The Pun. Sub rumble and plosives otherwise drive the preamp model into IMD and pump the compressors.

Clean lead

One chain serves the whole vox_ld_bus. Slots 1–6 are the manual chain. Slots P1, P2 and F come from the guides and are optional.

soothe2 Depth above 3.5

The blueprint allows Depth up to 4.5. The deep dive warns that Depth above 3.5 sounds flat and pushed back. The oeksound manual gives no fixed range. Depth depends on input level and mode, and 3.0 in Soft is not 3.0 in Hard. Start at 2.5. Use Delta to hear what it removes. Go higher only on bright screams.

Scream-only tracks

The JST guide pushes JW BG-Vocals to 5–8 dB GR on dedicated scream tracks. The page now uses it there. Metal mixers crush screams hard: Mark Mynett's vocal arrived with about 20 dB of series compression before the mix. Joey Sturgis puts heavy compression on each vocal track. Keep the shared bus at the manual 3–5 dB.

Hard mode artifacts

Hard mode clamps fast. If the vocal pumps or flanges, switch back to Soft.

Space on screams

David Bendeth put little reverb on screams in Bring Me The Horizon's “Shadow Moses”. He used a 1/8 delay on the screamed sections instead, plus a little slap set at 80 ms on the screaming parts. Choruses got a small room plus a 1/4 delay for length. Starting point: delays repeat in the gaps, while reverb tails sit under the next scream.

The template follows this. The plate send sits on vox_ld and vox_ld_dbl only. Screams keep the slap and the delay throws on vox_ld_bus.

Doubles & harmonies

Lead double

Do not double every line. Erik Ron skips doubles on many parts. A single lead reads closer and clearer in verses. Save the double for choruses and big lines.

The lead has one double, vox_ld_dbl. It thickens the lead, so it sits inside vox_ld_bus beside vox_ld, not with the BGVs. Joe Rickard sends the lead and its double to one bus so they hit one compressor together. Taylor Larson and the Nail The Mix vocal FAQ also glue doubles to the lead. No source found puts lead doubles in a BGV bus.

Pan the double center and tuck it under the lead. Joey Sturgis keeps only the occasional double in the center, reinforcing the lead, and pans the rest of the backgrounds to the sides. Mike Senior (Sound On Sound): a centered double holds up in mono better than a panned one. A lower double reads as one singer. At equal level it reads as two.

Widen the double in choruses. Eyal Levi (Chelsea Grin) runs his single mono double through Soundtoys MicroShift for a wide, chorus-like texture. The installed equivalent is MetaTune Doubler on vox_ld_dbl. At Wet/Dry 100% only the two new voices play, and Width sets their spread. Automate it on in choruses and off in verses, so verses stay focused and choruses go huge.

Keep the track compression light. The double already runs through the full vox_ld_bus chain, so a full lead chain on the track would compress it twice. Rickard uses about half the lead compression on the double and cuts its breaths. Andrew Wade de-esses only the doubles, hard, so the lead keeps its top end.

Low-pass the doubles. The muffled top end pushes them behind the lead, like they sing into a pillow. The lead keeps the air and the diction, so the vocal still sits on top in big choruses (WAP house rule, no source).

Breaking Benjamin's background vocals on Dark Before Dawn come from the band's other singers, per Ben Burnley. Those are BGVs: they go in vox_bgv_bus, not with the doubles.

Scream doubles

Keep the main scream on vox_scrm, center. Add a hard L/R pair, vox_scrm_dbl_l and vox_scrm_dbl_r, in vox_scrm_dbl_bus. The pair is a texture layer under the main scream, not a second lead. Michael Lessard pulls his scream layers down as a texture layer and glues them with compression. Buster Odeholm sends his panned layers to a pan bus with its own compressor.

Josh Schroeder keeps verses narrow and pans the stacked layers wide in breakdowns. He fades the extra layers out at transitions. Lessard quad-tracks screams: two mid takes and two high takes, each pair hard L/R, all real separate takes. For a quad, add the second pair as two more tracks inside vox_scrm_dbl_bus, with the same width 0%.

Use the layers per section, not on every line. Mute or automate them where the main scream must stand alone.

Doubles do not key the spotlight

Only vox_ld and vox_scrm send to sc_spotlight. The doubles come in on choruses and breakdowns, where the guitars need to be biggest. If they fed the key, they would push the instruments down further right there.

Harmony pairs

Harmonies are real L/R pairs. Both pairs sit in vox_harm_bus, inside vox_bgv_bus. vox_harm_bus is only a level fader: no plugins, no sends. The shared vox_bgv_bus chain and sends process every pair. Taylor Larson gives doubles and harmonies the same chain with different pans.

The Nail The Mix panning guide puts two harmony parts at 40–60% L/R. For stacks it puts the low and high harmonies near 30%, with wider layers out at 70% L/R. A Soundfly Flypaper tip pans low harmonies about 35% and high harmonies about 75%. The 40% / 65% split is a pick between those two sources, not a quoted value. Each layer gets its own width, so the stack does not pile up at one position.

Mike Senior: give each pair equal levels, or the image goes lopsided. Hard-panned layers drop about 3 dB in mono, so check mono.

The plate send and the spotlight key leave from the tracks, not the bus. vox_ld and vox_ld_dbl each send post-fader to fx_vox_plate, so the double shares the lead's space and the screams stay dry. vox_ld and vox_scrm each send pre-fader 0 dB to sc_spotlight. vox_scrm_dbl_bus reaches the slap and the delay only through vox_ld_bus.

Leveler on sc_spotlight

A scream hits the key much harder than a sung line. Without a leveler, the band ducks deeper under screams than under cleans. One compressor on the hub evens the key before it reaches the duckers. The hub sends leave pre-fader, post-FX, so the leveler acts on every key. This slot is reasoning, not a sourced rule.

    Insert it first, then set each ducker's threshold for its target GR. A clean chorus and a scream then duck the band by about the same amount (reasoning). The hub has Master/Parent Send off, so the leveler is never heard.

Delay throws

  1. Automate the send

    The send vox_ld_bus → fx_vox_dly sits at -inf. Draw send-level automation on it.

  2. Raise it on throw words

    Lift the send only on the phrases you want repeated.

  3. Let the duck work

    The return ducks 3–4 dB from sc_spotlight. Repeats bloom in the gaps.
