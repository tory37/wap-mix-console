Guitars: Rhythm, Lead, Clean

Goal

A dry, locked 7-string rhythm wall built on JST Toneforge Disruptor. Leads sing in the center. Cleans sit wide in the hall.

Rhythm guitars

Hard-pan the 7-string rhythms 100% L/R. Keep them 100% dry. JW BG-Guitars with Chug Slayer tames Drop G–Bb palm-mute boom.

Track layout: amp blend

The template builds an amp blend, not a quad. Both DIs sum into one pitch-shift bus, gtr_rhy_pitch. It feeds two amp sims. Amp A carries the tone. Amp B, the clarity amp, sits under it.

One amp sim instance on each amp track processes both hard-panned DIs. That works only in the sim's stereo input mode. Disruptor has an Input button with mono and stereo modes. Its stereo mode processes "two instruments simultaneously". Archetype Nolly, Gojira and Petrucci and Fortin Nameless each have a Stereo switch, because the original hardware takes a mono input. Set stereo on amp A, amp B and the pitch shifter. To check, solo gtr_rhy_l: the sound must stay left. If it spreads right, the sim is still in mono mode.

  1. Width 0% on the DIs

    The template sets the DIs to stereo pan mode with width 0%. Each DI is summed to mono, then panned. A stereo plugin on a DI cannot be half-muted by balance pan.

  2. Set amp A alone first

    Mute gtr_rhy_amp_b. Dial the full tone on amp A.

  3. Blend amp B under it

    Unmute amp B with its fader down. Raise it until the pick attack and note definition clear up, then back off a little. Amp A stays the louder amp (starting point).

  4. Check polarity

    Two amp sims can partly cancel. Flip amp B's polarity with the Invert Polarity button on its Disruptor cab and keep the fuller, tighter result. Full procedure in Amp B: clarity layer.

  5. Check mono

    Sum the mix to mono. The rhythm wall must not hollow out when amp B comes in.

Archetype Petrucci on gtr_rhy_pitch works only as a pitch shifter. Its Transpose knob is set per song. Songs with no shift: Transpose 0, or bypass the plugin.

Amp B: clarity layer

Amp B puts back the pick attack and string detail that amp A compresses away. It is a lower-gain, brighter amp tucked under amp A. Joey Sturgis, Taylor Larson, Carson Slovak and Buster Odeholm all blend a version of it. Sturgis puts the main amp at about 90% of the final tone. If you hear amp B as a separate sound, it is too loud.

Blend. Start amp B 8–12 dB under amp A (starting point). Raise it until it adds the detail you want, then pull it back slightly. Set the final level by ear in the full mix.

Phase. Two different sims on one DI always interact. Plugin latency and cab IR timing differ, so amp B can arrive a few samples off. The pitch shifter sits before both amps, so its latency adds equally to both and does not change this check.

  1. Set up the check

    Solo gtr_rhy_amp_a and gtr_rhy_amp_b. Bring amp B up to amp A's level. Sum the monitor to mono.

  2. Polarity first

    Flip amp B's polarity. Keep the setting with more low-mid body.

  3. Then single-sample nudges

    If both settings sound hollow, delay amp B one sample at a time and A/B each step. MAutoAlign or MTrackAlign can do this unverified.

  4. Back to blend level

    Pull amp B back down. Redo the check after any amp, cab or IR change.

Gating. Gate once on the DIs. Bypass the gates inside both sims. Two gates on two amps open and close at different times, so amp B's tails stick out. No source covers this case. It is a starting point.

Compression. None on amp B by default. Distorted rhythms need little compression, and Chug Slayer and JSTClip on the bus cover it. A lower-gain amp keeps more pick spikes. If amp B jumps out on hard picks, add 1–2 dB of light compression on amp B only (starting point).

  1. One player, both sides

    Adam Dutkiewicz has the songwriter track both rhythm sides on Killswitch Engage records.

  2. Flatten the low string for chugs

    Tune the low string about 15 cents flat for palm-mute sections. Hard picking pulls it sharp. Tune near zero for open chords.

  3. Punch in, keep the take

    Track full takes. Fix only the bad sections with punch-ins.

Zero reverb or delay on rhythms

Amp-sim reverb on hard-panned rhythms smears phase across the center. It masks the snare and the lead vocal. Turn it off in the amp and send nothing.

Vocal pocket: guitars keyed to the vocal

The rhythm wall is the main thing that masks the lead vocal. Cut the guitars only while the singer sings. Key the cut from sc_spotlight. Keep the cut in the mids. Chug lows and pick air stay untouched.

Default: the instrument_bus TDR Nova duck, keyed from sc_spotlight. It matches Joey Sturgis's setup and needs nothing on this bus. Upgrade, only if needed: the second soothe2 instance below, placed after slot 3. Run one pocket only.

Why this works

The oeksound manual describes this exact use: key the instruments from the lead vocal to carve space. Joey Sturgis keys his whole instrument bus from the vocal bus over about 300 Hz–6 kHz. That is why the instrument_bus duck is the default. Do one pocket only. If the instrument_bus sc_spotlight duck already clears the vocal, skip the soothe2 instance.

Lead guitars

The template pans gtr_ld at 10% L and gtr_ld_harm at 40% R, both width 0%. The lead stays near the center, where leads usually sit (center or 10–20% off). The harmony sits wider, so the pair stays separate and balanced. The lead has no sidechain key. Short melodic solos are ridden by hand: pull the rhythm fader down under a solo if it needs room. Keep pre-amp OD and modulation. Turn amp-sim reverb off. Turn delay off unless the riff needs it.

Shared returns, not amp reverb

The template has no lead key, so a lead-keyed tail duck needs your own routing. Without it, ride the send down on fast runs and let the tail bloom on held notes. Mark Mynett sends solos to the vocal effects at similar levels. The lead then sits in the same space as the singer.

Cleans, octave & ambient

The template pans gtr_cln_l at 65% L. The gtr_oct folder holds a doubled melody pair at 70% L/R (Octave melody pair). The wide pan clears the vocal pocket in quiet verses, where cleans play under the sung line. The instrument_bus duck covers this bus, so it has no key of its own. The hall gives them space.

Octave melody pair

Use gtr_oct for double-tracked melodies, such as an octave melody in the chorus. Play the same notes twice: two real takes, one on gtr_oct_l and one on gtr_oct_r.

  • Wide, not hard (reasoning). The rhythm doubles own 100% L/R. A melody in the same spot fights the wall: it gets buried, or it eats the wall when you push it. At 65–80% it reads as its own wide layer, and the center stays clear for the vocal. This matches the cleans at ±65%.
  • Why gtr_mel_bus, not gtr_ld_bus (reasoning). The lead bus is a center lead with plate and delay. This pair is a width layer. In gtr_mel_bus it gets the instrument_bus vocal duck and the hall.
  • Same tone, less gain (reasoning). Two takes with tight timing and the same tone on both sides read as one wide part. Less gain than the rhythms lets the notes ring through the wall.
  • Level (reasoning). Keep it under the vocal while the vocal sings. Ride it up only in gaps, where the hook has the spotlight.

Ambient space

The template sends gtr_mel_bus post-fader to fx_hall at -12 dB (starting point). For swells and clean arpeggios, JST Sky Box Cloud or Shimmer fits. Keep amp-sim reverb only when that space is part of the composition.
