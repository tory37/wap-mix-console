Orchestral Strings

Goal

Chorus strings hold sustained notes behind the guitars. Width carries them. fx_hall supplies the tail.

Lead or pad: pick one per section

A string part does one job at a time. The job sets its register, level and reverb.

RoleWhenWritingMix
PadUnder a busy or syncopated vocalSlow whole and half notes. Divisi chords inside the strings patch (its violin 2 and viola voices).Back. More hall. Mid cut on.
Counter-melodyIn gaps between vocal phrasesSimple line. High violin register, often in octaves.Forward. Less hall. Ride it up in the gaps.
Rhythmic driveBuilds, pre-chorusesFast 16th figures in unison octaves. Cellos an octave below.Drier. Shorts get less reverb than longs.

Soft textures disappear under distorted guitars. Harmonics and divisi tremolo make no impression there. Write firmer parts.

Register vs Drop G–Bb guitars & vocal

Drop G sits at 49 Hz. Drop Bb sits at 58 Hz. The guitar body owns the low octaves. The vocal owns the octave it sings in.

SectionWhere to write itWhy
ViolinsAn octave or more above the vocal melodyThe high register cuts through a dense mix. It stays out of the vocal octave.
Violas / violins 2 (inside the strings patch)Chord tones between vocal and violinsFills the middle without doubling the vocal line. No track of its own: orch_str_vln carries the ensemble patch, and the 7-string wall already fills the viola range (reasoning).

BassesUsually leave out. If used, double cellos an octave down and pan center.A double bass low E is 41 Hz. That is kick and bass guitar territory.
Starting point, not a sourced rule

"Above the vocal" and "above the chug body" are judgment calls from the sources below. No Red, Breaking Benjamin or FFTG engineer has published their string voicing.

Make LABS strings bigger

  1. Double in octaves

    Put the line in violins and an octave down in cellos. Octave doubling is how arrangers lift a second chorus.

  2. Layer a second library on long notes only

    Add BBC Symphony Orchestra or DSK Strings under LABS longs. Doubling everything sounds massive but loses detail. This is a starting point.

  3. Taper the note ends

    Players fade a long note out. Draw a short diminuendo on each release. Hard cut-offs sound sampled.

  4. Make each section full before it meets the band

    Mix bottom-up: cellos, then violins (violas live in the strings patch). Add weight first. Cut only where it clashes.

  5. Saturate lightly

    Tape saturation adds density. See slot 4 in the chain.

  6. Split shorts and longs

    Shorts read wetter than longs. Send shorts less hall than longs.

    The Mid cut clears the vocal zone. The Side lift pushes strings to the edges. Engineers report the string and synth clash with heavy guitars at 1–2 kHz. Set the HPF last, in context. A cello HPF at 150 Hz removes its warmth.

Depth: front and back

  1. Pads sit back

    More hall send. Lower fader.

  2. Counter-melodies sit forward

    Less send. Ride the fader up in vocal gaps.

  3. Automate the send, not only the fader

    Raise the hall send at phrase ends and rests. Pull it down under held notes.

  4. Clear the center

    Sound On Sound cut the mid of the string reverb to free the vocal. See fx_hall slot 2.

Fast songs

Mix magazine suggests zero pre-delay and no early reflections for strings that compete with vocals at fast tempos. The shared hall keeps 40–60 ms pre-delay. If fast strings smear, lower their hall send first. Do not change the shared hall for one part.

Dynamics through the song

  1. Ride before you compress

    Automate the notes that jump out. A ride adds no tone of its own.

  2. Bring a section in on a rise

    Fade the strings in. Then lift them for the second half of the section.

  3. Narrow verses, wide choruses

    Keep verse strings narrower. Open them on the chorus downbeat.

  4. Cut a bright note, not the whole part

    Find the harsh note with a boost. Then pull that band down only where the note plays.

Do not over-carve. Orchestral parts sound natural with some overlap. Cut only where you hear a clash.

JW BG-Keys has no Comp control

The JW manual lists Crush, Loud, GR, Push, Mix and Link. A "Comp 25–35%" value belongs to JST BG-Keys, a different plugin. Use Crush on JW BG-Keys.
