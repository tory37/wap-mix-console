Drums & drum_bus

Goal

Punchy kick, cracking snare, tame cymbals. drum_bus peaks at -9 to -7 dBFS.

Source setup

Source: GGD Modern & Massive plus Invasion triggers at Max Turbo unverified.

  1. One snare shell

    Keep a single natural snare. No multi-snare stacking.

  2. Sample-align triggers

    Align the Invasion snare trigger to the shell. Ghost notes stay organic.

  3. Meter the GGD output sends

    The template sends each GGD output pre-fader at -10 dB. Those sends are the GGD trim. Play the loudest section and check that the kit tracks peak at -12 to -10 dBFS. If they do not, move the GGD master output, not the sends.

  4. Skip close-mic gates and compressors

    GGD at Max Turbo already controls dynamics. Bleed is a separate choice in GGD's mixer.

  5. Check snare bottom polarity

    Inside GGD, play snr_top and snr_bot together. Flip the bottom mic's polarity and keep the version with more body. If the kit has no polarity switch for the bottom mic, use the REAPER Phase button on snr_bot unverified.

  6. Feed the crush from shells only

    The crush feed is kick_sum, snr_top, snr_bot, tom_1, tom_2 and tom_flr. kick_sub is excluded: its sub energy makes the crush bus pump. No triggers. No overheads. No rooms.

The template sets no pans on the drum buses. GGD outputs are stereo pairs, so the pans above come from GGD's mixer. Set them there, or narrow a track with its width control. Pick drummer view or audience view and keep it for the whole kit.

kick_bus

M&M Sum/Sub + Invasion trigger.

  1. 1

    ReaEQ

    REAPER EQ
    HPF
    45–50 Hz, 24 dB/oct (two stacked High Pass bands) unverified
    Punch
    +2 to +3 dB @ 75–85 Hz, Bandwidth 1.0 oct (about Q 1.4)
    Boxiness
    -4 to -5 dB @ 280–350 Hz
    Beater
    +2.5 to +3.5 dB @ 3.2–4 kHz

    The bass owns 49–70 Hz: the Drop G–Bb root lives there. The kick punches above it at 75–85 Hz, and the sc_kick duck opens the bass on each hit. The 45–50 Hz HPF stops the kick from stacking a sub under the low G. Mynett says to avoid kick boosts below 75 Hz, and he ties the kick HPF to pattern speed. These songs are mid-tempo, so a lower HPF does not smear (reasoning from his rule). The 280–350 Hz cut and the 3.2–4 kHz beater boost match published metal practice (Mynett: cut 250–450 Hz, attack near 3.8 kHz). Low priority: Mynett also lifts about 8 kHz for fast double kick. These songs rarely need it.

    Alternatives SLATEFG-S SLATESD-PE1 low trick: 60 Hz, Boost 4.5, Atten 3.5 unverified JSTJST EQ
  2. 2

    GClip

    GVST CLIP OPTIONAL
    Clip
    Touch point × 0.89 (1 dB) to × 0.84 (1.5 dB)
    Example
    Touch 25% (-12 dBFS) > Clip 22–21%
    Softness
    Raised (soft knee)
    Target
    Beater spikes
    2x Oversample
    ON

    Tames the click spike so the sub can sit louder. Clip is a percent of full scale: 100% is 0 dBFS. The kick peaks near -12 to -10 dBFS, so the touch point sits near 25–32%. A fixed percent does nothing at that level. Lower Clip until the line just meets the tallest spikes. Multiply that percent by 0.89 to 0.84. Only the spikes should cross the line. If the body crosses, back off. GClip has no GR meter: the gap between the line and the peaks is the reduction. Use the GClip float tool (Alt G).

    Alternatives JSTJST Clip

snr_bus

Single snare + Invasion trigger.

  1. 1

    ReaEQ

    REAPER EQ
    HPF
    100–120 Hz, 24 dB/oct (two stacked High Pass bands) unverified
    Body
    +2 to +3 dB @ 200–220 Hz
    Ring
    -3 dB @ 550–700 Hz (sweep to find it)
    Crack
    +2 dB @ 4–5 kHz
    Air
    +1.5 dB @ 8 kHz

    Body and crack up, ring out. The HPF sits higher than a standard-tuning mix. Snare energy below 100 Hz only masks the kick and the Drop G bass. Mynett (Sound On Sound) puts metal snare HPF at 110–170 Hz. Start at 110 Hz and raise it until the body thins. Crack sits at 4–5 kHz: Mynett puts snare attack at 4–8 kHz, and Nail The Mix puts crack near 5 kHz. It also stays clear of the guitar and vocal presence near 3 kHz.

    Alternatives SLATEFG-S JSTJST EQ
  2. 2

    GClip

    GVST CLIP
    Clip
    Touch point × 0.84 (1.5 dB) to × 0.75 (2.5 dB)
    Example
    Touch 25% (-12 dBFS) > Clip 21–19%
    Softness
    0% (hard clip)
    Target
    Stick spikes
    2x Oversample
    ON

    Essential. A rimshot spike lasts under 2 ms. Shaving it frees headroom with no loss of punch. The snare peaks near -12 to -10 dBFS, so the touch point sits near 25–32%. Lower Clip until the line just meets the tallest spikes, then multiply. If the body crosses the line, back off. Use the GClip float tool (Alt G).

    Alternatives JSTJST Clip (adds crack) SLATEHeatwave, Drive 35–45% as a starting point (extra body)

tom_bus

Filter each tom on its own track first. One bus EQ cannot filter the rack and floor toms at different points. Put a ReaEQ HPF on tom_1 and tom_2 at 75 Hz (rack) and on tom_flr at 55 Hz (floor). Mynett scales tom filters by drum size.

    Clears mud and brings stick attack forward.

room_bus

  1. 1

    ReaEQ

    REAPER EQ
    HPF
    150–185 Hz
    LPF
    4–6.5 kHz
    Cut
    -3 dB @ 300 Hz

    Keeps the room out of the low end and the cymbal wash. A crushed room at 100 Hz smears the kick under Drop G bass. Mynett high-passes the room at 185 Hz to protect kick punch. Start the LPF near 6.5 kHz for a big, open kit, and lower it toward 4 kHz if the cymbals wash. Mynett used a gentle 2 kHz, 6 dB/oct LPF for a darker, tighter room. That is darker than this sound needs (reasoning).

Parallel & plate returns

Both returns sum into drum_bus. Placement rules live on Routing.

fx_snr_plate

  1. 1

    Lustrous Plates

    LiquidSonics VERB
    Plate
    Steel
    Damper (reverb length)
    Short: heavy damping. The Damper has no seconds scale. Set it by ear.
    Pre-delay
    8–12 ms
    Send from snr_bus
    -12 dB (template)
    Low cut / High cut
    220 Hz / 7 kHz

    Short bright tail that cuts through the guitars. Automate it up in breakdowns. Published metal snare plates run 0.5–0.85 s with 7–11 ms pre-delay. Fast double-kick parts blur with a longer tail. LiquidSonics says real plates run from just under 1 s to about 5 s, so damp hard. If the tail still blurs, shorten it with the built-in Gate. Pre-delay starting point: 8–12 ms. Stretch the tail only in half-time sections.

    Alternatives JSTSky Box, Plate OTHERSSL PlateVerb

fx_crush

Fed post-fader at 0.0 dB by kick_sum, snr_top, snr_bot, tom_1, tom_2 and tom_flr. Not kick_sub: sub energy makes the crush bus pump. The template sets the fx_crush return fader to -16 dB. That return fader is the blend.

  1. 1

    VM FG-401

    Slate COMP
    Ratio
    About 10:1 (starting point)
    Target GR
    12–18 dB
    Blend
    Return fader under dry kit, start -14 to -18 dB (template: -16 dB)

    Adds density and sustain. The dry kit keeps the transient. A documented modern-metal crush ran about 18 dB GR, fed by kick, snare and toms, never cymbals. Heavier GR at a lower blend gives density with less transient smear.

    Alternatives SLATEFG-116 Vintage, All-Buttons (not in Slate's docs; use only if your build shows it) SLATEVBC FG-MU, -8 to -12 dB GR JSTJW BG-Drums, Pumpin' STOCKReaComp 20:1

drum_bus master

Peak target: -9 to -7 dBFS.

  1. 1

    ReaEQ

    REAPER EQ
    Low
    +1 dB @ 80 Hz
    Low-mid
    -1.5 dB @ 320 Hz
    Top
    +1 dB @ 10 kHz

    Broad kit tilt. Small moves only. The low lift sits in the 75–85 Hz kick window, above the 49–70 Hz range the bass owns.

    Alternatives JSTJST EQ SLATECS-EQ
