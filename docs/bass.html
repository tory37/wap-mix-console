The 180 Hz split

Treat the bass as two instruments: a mono sub and a biting mid guitar.

Why 180 HzDetail
Kick windowKick punch sits at 50–70 Hz, resonance to 100–120 Hz. The sub band holds the bass fundamentals below the low-mid box.
Guitar chugPalm-mute cab thump lives at 120–160 Hz. A 180 Hz ceiling keeps the sub out of it.
Mono safeBelow 150–200 Hz the ear cannot localize. Sum it to mono and compress it flat.
Note rangeDjinnBass plays down to G0 in Kontakt naming (C3 = MIDI 60). That is 49 Hz. Bb is 58 Hz. The bass sits in the same octave as the Drop G 7-string. Its fundamentals overlap the 50–70 Hz kick punch, so the SC_KICK duck matters.
Common rangeSplit points in metal guides run 100–300 Hz. 180 Hz sits inside it.

Zero-latency split & polarity check

  1. Use minimum-phase filters

    24 dB/oct: TDR Nova HP/LP Slope, or two stacked ReaEQ pass bands. They replace ReaFIR. Zero latency, sharp sub transients.

  2. Play a sustained low note

    Keep BASS LOW and BASS HIGH both active.

  3. Flip polarity on one band

    Use the track Phase button, or JS: Utility/chanmix2 Left Phase and Right Phase = Invert.

  4. Keep the thicker setting

    Listen at 180 Hz. Pick the fuller result.

No FX sends on BASS LOW

Zero reverb, delay or chorus. Wide lows cause wash and phase cancellation.

Release vs tempo

At 200 BPM a 16th note lasts 75 ms. A ducker release over 80 ms never recovers on fast double kick.

Set the bass level against the vocal

  1. Lock kick and bass first

    Kick and bass should hit together as one low-end event. The SC_KICK duck keeps the kick on top.

  2. Dovetail bass top with guitar bottom

    Bendeth matched the bass top end to the guitar bottom end. Where the guitar HPF ends, the bass grit takes over.

  3. Check the vocal last

    Play a chorus. Move BASS BUS ±1 dB. Keep the lowest level that still feels heavy. Bendeth: one dB too much bass makes the vocal disappear.
