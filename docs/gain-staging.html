Gain Staging & Top-Down Calibration

Goal

Feed every analog-modeled plugin -18 dBFS RMS (-12 to -10 dBFS peaks). Faders stay at 0.00 dB unity.

Why gain stage

Analog-modeled plugins expect -18 dBFS RMS. That equals about -12 to -10 dBFS peaks. This applies to:

Slate VCC Coffee The Pun VBC FG-Grey JW/JST Bus Glue

The Slate manuals state the reference. VCC: 0 VU = -18 dBFS (1 kHz sine). Revival: 0 VU at a -18 dBFS peak sine. VTM ships with a -15 dB Calibration Level. Per the VTM guide, a lower Calibration Level drives the tape harder, so the default reads about 3 dB cooler than a -18 dB calibration. Hold the targets below and the drive knobs act as labeled.

Hot input

Raw tracks at -1 dBFS overdrive saturation too early. The print_bus compressor then slams before you start mixing.

Why the stages climb (reasoning)

Faders stay at unity, so every folder adds its children. Similar, uncorrelated sources sum by about 10 × log10(n) dB: two add about 3 dB, three about 4.8 dB. Peaks that land together can add up to 20 × log10(n). Bus compressors take a little off. This is our own math, not a vendor figure.

Work back from the master. Max #2 starts at a -2.5 dB Threshold for -2.0 dBTP. GClip parks at 71% (-3.0 dBFS) and should shave 1–2 dB, so print_bus peaks at -2 to -1. instrument_bus at -3.5 plus vocal_master at -6.5 sums to about -1.7. Three instrument groups at -8 sum to about -3.2. vox_ld_bus at -8 plus the BGVs and returns gives about -6.5. Two loud tracks at -11 sum to about -8, which closes the loop at the raw-track target.

Stage 01 procedure

  1. Park faders

    Set all track and bus faders to 0.00 dB unity. One exception: the template sets the fx_crush return fader to -16 dB. That return sits after its plugins, so it sets the blend, not the plugin input.

  2. Bypass track FX

    Hear the raw source only.

  3. Trim clip gain

    Drag the top edge of each audio item. Or press F2 and set Take volume.

  4. Turn down VSTi outputs

    Lower the master output on DjinnBass, ANA 2 and LABS. Leave the GGD output alone. The template's 16 GGD output sends sit at -10 dB, and they are the GGD trim. Do not trim GGD again: two trims stack and leave the kit tracks too low.

  5. Meter the GGD output sends

    Play the loudest section and check that the kit tracks peak at -12 to -10 dBFS. If they do not, move the GGD master output, not the -10 dB sends.

  6. Read the meter

    Stop when the MCP peak readout shows -12.0 to -10.0 dBFS on the loudest chorus.

Fast path: Normalize Items

An alternative to steps 3 and 5. Normalize sets item volume, before FX, in one move. Faders stay at 0.00 dB. Audio items only.

  1. Select the items

    Select the raw audio items. Right-click an item and choose Item processing > Normalize items (Peak/RMS/LUFS). Or press F2 and click Normalize in Item Properties.

  2. Set Peak to -10 dBFS

    Use Peak mode, the top of the -12 to -10 dBFS target. The target is a peak target. One loud spike can leave the rest below -10, but never above it. That is safe for saturation. Do not use the LUFS or RMS modes here.

  3. Keep the mono option off

    Leave Adjust mono media items an additional -3dB off. With it on, mono items land at -13 dBFS, below the target.

  4. Check by ear and meter

    If a track sounds low after the spike, nudge its item volume up. Stop at -10.0 dBFS peak.

Several items on one track

Comps and song sections: select all items on the track and choose Normalize to loudest item. One gain applies to all, so verse/chorus dynamics stay. Normalize each item separately puts every item at -10 and flattens the song.

Whole session in one run: ReaScript

The script does Normalize to loudest item on each track. Per track, one gain moves all items so the loudest item peaks at -10 dBFS. Clip-gain rides stay. It skips MIDI items and silent items. It runs on every track. One undo step reverts it. Two switches sit at the top of the file: ONLY_SELECTED_TRACKS and SKIP_DI_TRACKS (names that end in _di). Both are off by default.

Download wap_normalize_tracks_common_gain.lua

  1. Load the script

    Choose Actions > Show action list > New action > Load ReaScript. Select the downloaded file.

  2. Assign a key (optional)

    In the action list, select the script. Click Add under Shortcuts and press a key.

  3. Run it

    Run the action. The ReaScript console shows the gain per track and the skipped tracks. Press Ctrl+Z to undo.

Guitar and bass DIs into amp sims

DI level is the input gain of JST Toneforge Disruptor and DjinnBass 1. Normalize DIs before you dial the amps. Every song then hits the amp at the same level, and one amp setting works for the whole record. If the amps are already dialed, do not normalize the DIs (set SKIP_DI_TRACKS to true), or the tone changes.

VSTi and MIDI

No audio item exists to normalize. Lower the instrument output knob or use Slot 1 JS: Volume Adjustment.

Four REAPER rules

  1. Park all faders at 0.00 dB unity

    Never pull a fader down to fix a hot DI, vocal or synth preset. REAPER faders have maximum resolution near 0.00 dB.

  2. Trim pre-FX with clip gain or VSTi out

    Drag item top edges or use F2 Item Properties. Adjust VSTi output knobs or Slot 1 JS: Volume Adjustment. Target -12 to -10 dBFS peak.

  3. Watch summing headroom

    Expect instrument_bus at -4 to -3 dBFS and vocal_master at -7 to -6 dBFS. print_bus then receives -2 to -1 dBFS peaks. Much hotter: go back to the raw-track trims, not the faders.

  4. Gain-match every plugin on/off

    Match bypassed and active loudness on Coffee The Pun, FG-116 and JW/JST Bus Glue. Louder always sounds better. Gain-matching removes that bias.

Calibrate the print_bus top-down. Park the chain first. Then set compression on the first loud chorus.

Step B: first-chorus calibration

  1. Loop a loud chorus

    Keep Drums, Bass and Rhythm Guitars active.

  2. Pull down VBC FG-Grey Threshold

    Stop when the needle hits 2–3 dB GR on snare and kick hits.

  3. Gain-match Makeup

    Bypassed and active must sound equally loud.

  4. Check JST Maximizer #1

    It must still read 0.0 dB GR.
